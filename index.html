How it works

Six steps from first message to handover.

  1. Scoping call

    We agree what the project needs, which network it lives on, and what it must never do.

  2. Written scope and quote

    Deliverables, timeline and price in writing, signed off before work starts.

  3. Build and test

    Contracts are tested against the chain's real contracts, including deliberate attempts to break them.

  4. Deploy together

    You deploy and fund from your own wallets with a step-by-step checklist. I check every value on-chain before anything is funded.

  5. Verify and publish

    Source published with a check anyone can run against the chain, explorer verification wherever the explorer supports it, and every address published on your site.

  6. Handover

    An admin guide, emergency pause steps, and the full source in your own GitHub repository.

Ground rules

What you can count on, and what I won't do.

Your keys, your funds

I never hold private keys, seed phrases or project funds. Every deployment and transfer is signed from your wallets.

No price promises

Your pages say what the token is, what it isn't, and the risks. No guaranteed returns and no hype copy.

The law is yours to check

You decide where your sale is allowed and who can take part. I can build in exclusions, but I don't give legal advice.

Testing isn't an audit

My testing is thorough, but it isn't an independent audit. For real money, get one.

Open source by default

Contracts are published so anyone can check them against the chain.

Plain words

Before you sign anything, you'll know exactly what each contract does and who controls it.

Tell me what you're launching

A short message is enough to start: what the project is, roughly when you want it live, and whether you need a sale.
