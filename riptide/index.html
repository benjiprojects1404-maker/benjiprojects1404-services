Riptide

Every minute, Riptide asks Arc's Uniswap pools what a $10 and a $1,000 trade would really return, compares that with Binance and Coinbase (or, for ARGUS, with Arc's other Uniswap pools), and logs every gap after fees. It is a paper-trading experiment: it never trades, holds no funds and has no keys.

How it counts, and what it leaves out

  • Arc prices are real quotes from Uniswap v3's QuoterV2, so they already include the pool fee and the slippage for that trade size.
  • Exchange prices are the best bid and ask: Binance BTC/USDC and ETH/USDC where reachable, otherwise Coinbase BTC/USD and ETH/USD (USDC converts 1:1 to USD there), and Coinbase EURC/USDC (Binance has no EURC market). Binance blocks some server regions, including the one Riptide runs in, so Coinbase is usually the reference.
  • ARGUS is compared Arc against Arc: it has no exchange listing, so Riptide buys in Arc's Uniswap v3 pool and sells the same tokens in a Uniswap v4 pool (and the reverse), using chained real quotes. Both legs settle on Arc, so there's no exchange fee and no transfer, only the two pools' fees (about 1% each) and two swaps' gas. It only watches the v4 pools with real liquidity and normal fees; most of the ~170 ARGUS/USDC v4 pools are empty or charge 20–50%.
  • Rug alarm: on a DEX a rug pull happens in a single transaction, far faster than any stop-loss can react, so Riptide doesn't pretend to exit. It flags the warning signs instead and pauses the pair, because a huge "gap" during a rug is a trap, not an opportunity.
  • Costs subtracted: the exchange's taker fee (assumed 0.1% Binance, 0.6% Coinbase; your own tier may be lower) and Arc network fees at the live gas price.
  • Not counted: moving funds between Arc and an exchange. Withdrawal and bridge fees, and the minutes or hours a transfer takes, are usually the real barrier, so a "would trade" here is an upper bound, not a promise.
  • cirBTC is not BTC and WETH is not ETH: they're Circle's and a bridge's wrapped versions on Arc, priced against the real thing.
  • The WETH pool on Arc is very small, so its $1,000 numbers mostly show slippage in a shallow pool.
  • Read-only and public. No wallet, keys or exchange accounts are involved. Not financial advice.
